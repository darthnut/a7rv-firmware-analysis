The short answer

Why a bracket burst is slower than a plain burst

Imaging costs the same in both modes: 9 fields of exposure and readout. A bracket is slower for two separate reasons, and which one you hit depends on how long the stack is.

1

Every frame waits for the lens

stacks up to ~44 frames

A plain burst never moves focus, so the next exposure can start as soon as the last readout ends. In a bracket, the camera has to move the focus group one step and wait for it to stop before it may expose. That adds 11 fields to every frame: 1 to compute and send the move, about 6 while the lens physically travels, and a fixed 4-field settle. The firmware runs this move strictly before the exposure, never during the previous readout, because the next move is only kicked off once the previous frame finishes. 9 + 11 = 20 fields, so the frame rate drops from 6.6 to 3.0 frames per second.

2

Long stacks fill the buffer

past ~44 frames

For RAW-L files saved to both the card and the PC, the buffer holds about 44 frames at full speed. After that each new frame has to wait for space while earlier frames are still being developed and written, and the cadence falls to whatever the write path can sustain: about 36 fields (0.6 s) saving to the card only, and 95–116 fields (1.6–1.9 s) when tethered and saving to both. The stall is in the develop subsystem (av-cam.bin), and its buffer size is set at run time, so the firmware has no constant here to change.

Ruled out on the bench

  • Tethering: 20 fields both tethered and card-only.
  • Flash at low power (1/200): same 20 fields and the same cliff at about frame 45.
  • Exposure time: the shutter is far shorter than the frame interval.

Still open: older card-only stacks ran at 26 fields, not 20. The leading suspect is a flash-recycle interlock (SB_CHARGE lock, file 0x2213CC) adding fields at higher flash power. The mechanism is in the code; whether it explains the 26 is not yet tested.

What would actually help

  • Smaller focus step width. The lens moves less per frame, so the ~6 travel fields shrink.
  • A faster-focusing lens. Same effect on the travel time.
  • Keep stacks under ~44 frames, or push the cliff later with compressed RAW, a faster card, or saving to one destination.
  • Not the settle patch. Lowering the 4-field settle saves 2–3 fields at most and risks soft frames. Even with an instant lens the floor is about 15 fields per frame.

Body floor. 1 (send) + 1 (minimum state-1 poll) + 4 (settle) = 6 fields the body always spends. Even an instant lens could not beat about 15 fields (250 ms) per frame with this drive profile.

No overlap. The move cycle (states 0–2) strictly precedes imaging (state 3). The next kick comes from the next frame's capture-init, and no code path runs them in parallel.
